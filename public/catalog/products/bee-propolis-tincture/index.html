About this tincture

Propolis, often called bee glue, is the resin honeybees gather from tree buds and bark and work together with beeswax to seal and protect their hive. Ours comes from our own beehives in Manteca. We draw its resins out with high-proof grain alcohol: the raw propolis is frozen, cracked into rice-sized flakes, and steeped for at least four weeks, shaken every day, then strained twice. What comes out is a dark amber tincture with a bitter, piney, balsamic taste that briefly numbs the tongue. People around the world have kept propolis in the cupboard for centuries, most often reaching for it during cold-and-flu season and for a scratchy throat, and beekeepers have long dabbed it on minor scrapes.

How much to use
  • Adults: Start with 5–10 drops, once or twice a day
  • First time: Try 2–3 drops, and test a drop on your inner arm
  • Minor scrapes: Clean the skin, then dab on 1–2 drops once a day. It stings for a moment, then dries to a thin film.

Do not use if you are allergic to bees, honey or pollen. Propolis can cause a skin rash in some people: test a drop on your inner arm and wait a day first. Not for deep, dirty or infected wounds, bites or burns: see a doctor. Contains about 72% alcohol: not for children. Ask a doctor first if you are pregnant, nursing or take medication, especially blood thinners. Flammable.

What is propolis?

Propolis, sometimes called bee glue, is the sticky resin honeybees collect from tree buds and bark and mix with beeswax. They use it to seal gaps and coat the inside of the hive, where it helps keep the colony clean. A tincture pulls those resins into alcohol so you can take propolis by the drop.
